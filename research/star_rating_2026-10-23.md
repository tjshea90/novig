# One star rating, fitted to the data (ST1, Tj 2026-10-23, chat t1792793515523-b161)

## What changed
`BetRating` stays 1-5 stars in half steps; it is now ONE score of six parts (coverage 20, agreement 20, edge 25, sharp book 25, consensus 10, **type of bet +-15**) plus two rules. The separate "-> x after your history" stars are gone from both scanners (`ProfitProfile.adjustedStars` returns the shown stars; nothing is taken off twice). Profile lookups: `ProfitProfile.typePoints` (league|kind|side, league|kind, kind|side, league, kind, side, lead bucket; shrunk by n/(n+100), weighted by specificity, 0.8 points per ROI percent, clamped to +-15). Lead time adds +3 (<= 6 h), 0 (6-24 h), -4 (> 24 h). Two companies or fewer with no sharp book among them: at most 2 stars (was: 1 company 1.5, 2 companies 3).

## Evidence used
1. Tj's bet history (`profit_profile.json`, 1,337 bets, regenerated by `tools/profit-profile.py`), `profitable_types_2026-10-13.md`: player-prop overs lose (-15% ROI, t -3.0), game-total overs +21% (t 1.1), unders -16% (t -2.0), 24 h+ bets give the close away.
2. The engine's graded lines, Release `engine-dataset` `lines-2026-10-16..25` (20,673 rows; 808 with edge > 0 and a close). CLV = close_fair / cost - 1 against SGO Pinnacle/Circa (91% of those closes) or Novig's last trades:
   - Edge: 0-2% +0.4% (t 1.5), 2-4% +1.9% (t 3.5), 4-6% +1.6% (n 49). Edge up = CLV up, but not linear above 4%.
   - Agreement share (companies saying +EV): <=50% -1.0%, 50-75% -1.0%, 75-99% +0.5%, 100% +1.1% (t 1.8).
   - Lead: <1 h +1.9%, 1-3 h +0.8%, 3-6 h +3.1%, 6-13 h +1.1%, 13-24 h +0.7%, >24 h +0.2% (n 278, t 0.4).
   - Kind: props +1.8% (t 5.7, n 429), team totals +1.7% (n 47), game totals -0.2%, spreads -0.5%, moneylines -0.6%. League: NHL +1.8% (t 5.5), NBA +1.5%, NFL/NCAAF ~0.
   - Side (props): overs +3.2% (n 171), unders +1.0%. NOTE: prop overs beat the close yet LOSE money in Tj's results (favorite-longshot bias; the close is not a free pass for longshot-priced overs). The rating therefore leans on the history ROI for type and on CLV for lead time and agreement, and does not add CLV points for overs.
   - 1-2 two-sided books: -1.0% to -1.7% (OLS t -1.7).
   - OLS on the 808 rows (CLV on edge, sharp, agree, <=2 books, lead>24h, kind, over): only `over` (+1.8, t 3.2) is clearly non-zero; `<=2 books` -1.7 (t -1.7), `prop` +1.5 (t 1.7). Sharp-agrees has no CLV signal among +EV rows (only 353 have it, and closes are Pinnacle's own price, so it is circular).
3. Existing: `propline_clv_first_run_2026-10-16.md` (listed bets +2.6% CLV, hidden -0.9%), `clv_consensus_2026-10-21.md` (close quality: Novig's last 3-10 trades is the noisiest close, -1.4% vs -0.3% for SGO Pinnacle).
## Caveats (say them)
- 808 closed +EV rows from ~10 days: t-statistics are modest, so the type term is capped at +-15 points and shrunk. ROI per type is noisier than CLV.
- Star buckets today: 3 stars CLV +1.2% (n 187), 4 stars -1.1% (n 44), 2-2.5 stars -0.8% (n 161): ordering is only roughly monotone; refit when 200+ closed rows per bucket exist.
## Outside sources (reputable only; none tests CLV against profit directly)
- Snowberg & Wolfers, "Explaining the Favorite-Longshot Bias: Is It Risk-Love or Misperceptions?", J. Political Economy 118(4), 2010 (https://www.nber.org/papers/w15923): longshots are overbet because of misperceived probabilities. Supports demoting longshot-priced overs and keeping the +150/+200 caps.
- Krieger (2015), Financial Services Review, market efficiency of NFL closing lines (https://openjournals.libs.uga.edu/fsr/article/view/3242): closing lines are close to efficient, so a bet that beats the close is the right yardstick.
- Practitioner sources (SharpAPI docs on CLV, vendor blogs) say only that CLV is meaningful against a sharp reference; not peer reviewed.
