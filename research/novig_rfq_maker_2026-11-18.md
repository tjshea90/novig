# Becoming a Novig parlay maker: how it works, what it can earn, what it risks, the plan (MK, Tj 2026-11-18)

Tj: "I want to become a parlay maker and try to profit from it. Research and build it."
Sources: Novig's RFQ v3 docs (mirror `api-docs`, `novig/docs.novig.com/api/rfq/*` and `api-reference/rfq/*`, changelog 2026-11-12), `fees.md` (parlay taker fee change of 2026-11-16), PropLine's parlay and SGP trial of 2026-11-17 (`propline_unused_features_2026-11-17.md`), the engine's own fair lines.

## 1. How a round works (docs)
1. A user builds a parlay of 2-20 legs (any market, main lines to props to futures, pregame or live). Novig opens a **3-second auction** and pushes `rfq_created {rfqId, outcomeIds, expiresTs, minWager}` to **every registered maker** on `GET /rfq/v3/ws` (channel `rfq`).
2. A maker answers `quote {rfqId, price, maxWager, clientId?}` or `decline`; no answer = the RFQ just closes. `price` is the **probability the parlay wins** (0.001-0.999; "0.45" = a $0.45 stake per $1.00 payout); `maxWager` is the biggest stake the quote covers (must reach `minWager`, the user's minimum or $10). **One quote per RFQ, the first stands.**
3. **The lowest price wins** (it pays the user most); a tie goes to the larger `maxWager`. Novig passes over a quote whose `maxWager` is under `minWager`, whose collateral the subaccount cannot cover, that is the maker's own RFQ, or on legs the maker rejected/missed a last look on in the past 2 minutes.
4. If the user accepts, the winner gets `quote_accepted {wager, price}` and has **1 second** to `confirm {confirmed: true}`. Silence, lateness or a dropped connection = rejected, and counts against the **confirm rate: 99% of last looks over 24 hours** or the maker is locked out of quoting for 5 minutes (counting restarts at zero afterwards).
5. The parlay settles as ONE position: any losing leg ends it at once (the maker keeps the whole pot); a void leg settles at its fair market value (a product of leg values, 0 to 1, floor 0.001, rounded to 3 decimals); a leg never pushes.
- **Money:** the user's stake `w`, the maker's collateral `k = w (1 - P) / P` (from the maker subaccount's balance), pot `= w / P`. The maker's EV per $1 of pot is **`P_quoted - p_true`**. A maker pays **no fee**; the taker pays `0.10 x w x (1 - P)` until 2026-11-18 and **`0.055 x (-ln P) x w` from 2026-11-19** (charged to the user at execution; maker credit does not apply to RFQs).
- Registration: `POST /rfq/v3/maker` with the subaccount's **`trading` key** (idempotent, returns `makerId`, up to a minute to propagate; needs verified KYC and no self-exclusion). Socket: `trading` key to quote/decline/confirm; a **`trading::read` key may connect and read**. `GET /rfq/v3/maker` (tier, confirm standing, lock), `GET /rfq/v3/collateral` (open collateral), `GET /rfq/v3/executions` (own fills and how they settled). Beta: every maker is `basic`.
- **What every maker can see for free (the key fact for a safe start):** `rfq_created` (legs, minimum stake), `rfq_closed`, and on channel `executions` an **`executed {offset, rfqId, wager, price}` for EVERY RFQ trade on Novig** (it never says which maker won). So without quoting anything a registered maker can log every parlay Novig is asked for, which ones traded, at what price and stake.

## 2. What it can earn (evidence so far)
PropLine's `/v1/parlay/price` returns **Novig's own parlay quote** next to the fair price built from the sharp books. On 2026-11-17, 8 two-leg moneyline parlays from different games: Novig's quote minus fair was **+0.7 to +2.0 points of pot** (0.1057 vs 0.0967, 0.2710 vs 0.2525, 0.3413 vs 0.3215...), i.e. the user's EV was -4.3% to -8.4% before the taker fee. That gap is the makers' margin: about **4-8% of the user's stake** per executed parlay if Pinnacle's no-vig line is the truth. Four of twelve combinations were not quoted at all (Novig declined the combination).
- So the business is real but thin and competitive: you win only by quoting the LOWEST price, so every point of margin you keep is a point you might lose the auction on, and you win exactly the auctions where your number is lower than the other makers' (**winner's curse**: if their information is better than yours, the auctions you win are the ones you misprice).
- The edge needs (a) an accurate probability for every leg, (b) independent legs (different games, no shared team: Novig's own rule), or a correlation model for same-game legs, (c) pregame legs only (no live prices), (d) volume: unknown. **Nobody outside Novig has published how many RFQs there are or how big the stakes are; that is what the shadow run measures.**

## 3. Risks (and how each is contained)
| Risk | Containment |
|:--|:--|
| Winner's curse / a wrong leg price | Quote only legs the engine prices from fresh multi-book fair lines (at least 3 books agreeing or a sharp book, age under 5 min, the same fair the auto-bet trusts, after its prop and thin-early cuts); start with a wide margin; widen on any adverse result |
| Same-game legs (correlation) | Decline every same-game combination until a correlation model has evidence (the SGP study logs the books' correlation factors) |
| Live (in-play) legs | Decline: the engine has no live fair for them |
| Collateral lock-up (a parlay can stay open for days; futures for months) | Cap one parlay's collateral, the total open collateral (read from `GET /rfq/v3/collateral`) and the share of the wallet; no futures; games starting within 7 days only |
| Last-look rejections (a 99% bar) | Quote only what will be confirmed; the last look confirms whenever the quote is still sound and never second-guesses on a price move of the legs (a rejection is a lockout and a worse standing) |
| Mixing with the engine's bets and bids (one subaccount, one wallet) | The maker's collateral comes out of the same balance: its cap counts bets and bids as already used (`cloudWalletUse`); the engine's own cash read sees collateral as spent |
| Fee schedule changes (11-19) and the beta's rules changing | Read the docs mirror weekly (`api-docs.yml`); every number in this file carries its date |
| A bug that quotes nonsense | Modes OFF / SHADOW / LIVE, **default OFF**; SHADOW never sends a quote; LIVE has hard caps and a kill (any `MAKER_LOCKED`, a standing under 99.5%, a day's loss over the cap, or `quote_unfilled maker_insolvent` stops it) |

## 4. The plan
- **MK2 (built here): the maker lives in the GitHub engine** (the phone only monitors). `gates`-free: its mode and numbers are a file on `cloud-config`, `rfq-maker.json` (`mode` OFF/SHADOW/LIVE, margin, caps), so an old phone build can never overwrite it and `engine_set`-style writes are plain commits.
- **MK3: SHADOW first.** The engine registers the maker (idempotent, with the trading key), opens the RFQ socket, subscribes `rfq` + `executions`, **sends nothing**, and journals every RFQ with: the legs, the fair price of each leg from the engine's scan, the parlay fair price, whether every rule would let it quote, the quote it WOULD have made, the RFQ's close, and the execution (price and stake) when one follows. A daily table (`tools/rfq_summary.py`) gives: RFQs a day, share priceable, share same-game, stake sizes, executed share, and **the executed price minus our fair price** (the market's margin), which is the number that decides whether quoting can earn and how wide to start.
- **LIVE only when the shadow data says so:** at least 200 priced executed RFQs with a median `executed - fair` above the margin plus a safety cushion, and the quote counterfactual (we would have won X% at margin m) in a table. Start with a $5 stake cap and a $15 total collateral cap, then widen by the class B steps in `research/review/ROUTINE.md`.
- Honest expectation: with a $200 wallet this is a **measurement and a small side business, not a second income**: collateral per $10 parlay at P = 0.2 is $40, and the margin is 1-2 points of a $50 pot.

## 6. First real run (SHADOW, 2026-11-18 03:07 UTC, the first thing that answers section 5)
- **Production serves the v3 RFQ routes and accepts the Vigilant subaccount as a maker.** `POST /rfq/v3/maker` answered with a `makerId` (the same on the second call: idempotent). The first socket upgrade, 5 s later, got `404` (the documented `MAKER_NOT_FOUND`: registration takes up to a minute to reach every server); the retry connected. `RfqSocket` already re-registers on a 404 and backs off.
- **The flow is far bigger than assumed: about 60 RFQs a second and about 1.7 executed parlay trades a second** (first 70 s: 4,340 RFQs seen, 123 trades). That is a few thousand trades an hour on Novig, so volume is not the scarce thing: pricing coverage is.
- **Priceable share 0.4% (17 of 4,340):** 62% a leg with no fair line in the engine's scan (the leg is a market or league the scan does not price, or a side with no outside fair), 33% more than 3 legs (the maker prices 2 to 3), 5% two legs in one game. Of the RFQs with 2 to 3 legs, about 92% still had a leg with no fair line.
- **The one trade we could price:** the market paid +0.56 points of pot over our fair; a quote at an 8% margin would have won 0 of 1. One trade proves nothing; the table needs 200+.
- **Consequences built the same day:** the journal would have been gigabytes a day, so it now keeps every priceable RFQ and every trade whole, a 1-in-25 sample of the rest, and a `TALLY` row every 5 minutes (RFQs by leg count, why declined, leg classes `priced` / `scanned` (the scan holds the outcome but no fair line) / `unscanned` (not in the scan at all), the market kinds of scanned legs, trade count/stake/legs); the pending window that matches a trade to its RFQ was 600 entries (10 s at this rate) and is now 2 minutes; a trade of an RFQ we could not price keeps its outcome ids so the legs can be resolved through Novig's public catalog and priced from history later.
- **What decides the next step:** (1) what the unpriced legs are (props? leagues the scan does not read? other sports?): widen the engine's fair coverage for the commonest kind; (2) the trades' stake distribution (the $10 minimum stake and our $12 cap: how many trades are within reach); (3) the executed-minus-fair margin table over 200+ priced trades; (4) who the trades are (1.7 a second looks like bots, which would also mean sharp competing makers: the winner's curse is then the main risk). LIVE stays off until (3) says so.

## 7. What the first 10 minutes of legs look like (`tools/rfq_legs.py`, the unsampled journal of run 44227894385, 32,682 RFQs, 960 trades; the catalog read 13 min later)
- **45% of RFQs hold an in-play leg** (a full NBA night at 03:07 UTC): the engine prices pregame only, so these are out of reach by design. Another 4% have a leg already gone from the open catalog.
- **Legs by league:** NBA 55%, NCAAB 10%, NHL 8%, NFL 7%, NCAAF 5%, ATP 2%; by type: MONEY 32%, player POINTS 14%, TOTAL 7%, SPREAD 7%, THREE_POINTERS_MADE 6%, REBOUNDS 6%, ASSISTS 5%. Players' props are about 40% of legs.
- **The trades** (960 in 10 min): stake median $9, p25 $5, p75 $19, p90 $56; **30% are 2-leg, 15% 3-leg, 55% have 4 to 20 legs**. The market is mostly bigger parlays than the maker prices; collateral for a $9 stake is w(1-P)/P: $15 at P = 0.37 (the median we priced), $170 at P = 0.05. **A $200 wallet limits this, not the flow**: the maker's return per collateral dollar is (price - fair)/(1 - price), about 2% at P = 0.2 and 8.7% at P = 0.5 for an 8% margin, so high-probability 2-3 leg parlays are the efficient ones.
- **Early market margin (25 priced trades, 23 of them 2-leg; too few to decide anything):** the market paid a median +1.53 points of pot over our fair (+3.9% relative, p25 +1.22, p75 +5.93); a quote at a 4% margin would have won 12 of 25, at 8% 11 of 25, earning +1.55 points of pot each at 8%.
- **Coverage is not yet measured.** That window straddled a cold engine (the scan result is empty or aging for the first ~10 minutes; priceable RFQs per minute went 22-36 at 03:08-03:11 and 0-3 afterwards, the 5-minute fair-age rule biting a stale index) and 8,498 of the 2-3-leg pregame RFQs had a leg with no fair line (NBA player props, NHL goals/assists, NFL/NCAAF game lines more than 2 days out, tonight's NBA game lines that were alternate numbers or not in the scan). **Read the next journal from a WARM job (run 44229304633, sampled, with TALLY rows) before building any coverage.**

## 8. The warm job (run 44229304633, 03:19-03:37 UTC, SAMPLED journal + TALLY rows) 
Numbers below use the TALLY rows (every RFQ counted, ~16,500 per 5 minutes, ~500 trades, $15-20k of user stakes) and the journal's priced trades; the leg tables of a sampled journal over-represent RFQs the maker could price, so only weights from TALLY rows are used for shares.
- **Where the RFQs go (share of RFQs, 3 warm windows):** a leg with no fair line 45-48%; more than 3 legs 33-34%; two legs in one game 13-17%; quoted (every rule passes) 0.2-0.6% (39, 83, 97 per 5 minutes: the engine's fair index fills as the scan warms). In-play legs are 45% of RFQs in the NBA-night window of the first run but only 6% of the sampled rows here (the journal keeps priced ones, so the tally is the truth: ask `legClasses`).
- **Leg classes per 5 minutes (all legs, all RFQs):** priced 17-23k, held by the scan but without a fair line ("scanned") 5-8k (player props: Three Pointers Made, Points, Rebounds, Assists, PRA: alternate numbers the books do not quote), **not in the scan at all ("unscanned") 31-38k**. The unscanned class is the biggest and not yet resolved by kind: resolve the sampled/traded outcome ids with `tools/rfq_legs.py` (catalog snapshot of 03:20 UTC saved in `research/rfq/`; ids only resolve while the markets are open).
- **Trades:** about 1.7 a second (475, 502, 536 per 5 minutes), mean stake $41, median $9; 2-leg 26-29%, 3-leg 15%, 4-leg 17-20%, 5-20 legs 35%. The share the engine could price grew with the scan's warmth: 6% -> 8% -> 10% (30, 41, 53 per window).
- **The margin the market pays (164 priced trades, 135 of them 2-leg):** median executed price minus our fair **+1.06 points of pot (+3.0% relative)**, p25 +0.66, p75 +4.54. Our quote at margin m would have WON: 0% 95% (156 of 164), 2% 70%, 4% 45%, 6% 39%, **8% 37%**, 10% 35%, 12% 34%; earning (if our fair is true) +0.53, +0.75, +1.08, +1.46, +1.81, +2.07 points of pot per win. The win share falls fast from 0 to 4% and then flattens: a third of the trades printed 12% or more above our fair. Two readings, to be separated by outcomes: (a) those are weak quotes by other makers (nobody competes on them) and a wide margin earns well; (b) our fair is wrong on those (legs with a thin or stale fair) and the winner's curse is hiding in that tail. **A quote at a wide margin (8-12%) wins a third of the priced trades, an engine cannot tell (a) from (b) from prices alone.**
- **What it could earn (if our fair is true), honestly:** at an 8% margin about 0.54 points of pot per priced RFQ it quotes; a $10 stake at P 0.37 is a $27 pot, so about $0.4 per win, tying $17 of collateral for hours. The wallet cap ($90 open collateral) holds about 5 such parlays at a time: roughly **$5-15 a day on a $200 wallet at best**, before any winner's curse. The market is far larger than the wallet; the wallet and the quality of the fair are the limits.
- **Next evidence (MK5):** realized outcomes of the TRADES (every trade has its price, stake and legs; priced ones and unpriced ones with `outcomeIds`): the aggregate P&L of the makers who won them, by leg count and price band, tells whether the market's prices are fair, with no model of ours in between. Needs leg results: Novig's public catalog showed FINAL events with markets still `OPEN`/`TBD` (checked 2026-11-18 03:40), so use the engine's own graders (ESPN/MLB/SGO/PropLine results) keyed by (league, event, market type, strike, side) from the saved catalog snapshot, or the catalog again once it settles; snapshots of the open catalog must be saved daily because ids vanish.
- **Next coverage (MK6), in order of volume per unit of work:** (1) up to 5-6 legs where the parlay's fair probability is high enough for the collateral caps (`maxLegs` 3 -> 6; the stake rule already declines what the caps cannot take); (2) two legs in one game (13-17%): the books' correlation factors are in the SGP study logs (`propline-sgp`), a parlay with exactly two same-game legs of game-line markets could use them; (3) alternate player-prop numbers: a ladder model through the books' quoted lines; (4) the unscanned legs by kind (read first).
