# The maker's prop fair from EVERY price the feeds carry (MK22, 2026-11-20)

**Trigger.** Tj 2026-11-20 00:26 UTC: "Propline and parlayapi both carry all kinds of props and alternate markets. The engine should carry fair odds on almost everything ... the engine can only price 56 out of 200000 rfqs ... Out of over 9000 trades, the engine could price 2. It is broken." He was right, and my first answer (the books only post a couple of strikes; extrapolate) was wrong about the cause.

**1. What PropLine really sends** (task `propcov` of `propline.yml`, run 44646110230, 00:28 UTC, three leagues; raw boards in the `propline-samples` Release, `board-<sport>-<market>.json.gz`). Per player and stat the books post, beside the main Over/Under:
- *Two-sided alternate rungs* (Hard Rock up to 33 strikes a player for NFL receiving yards, Bovada, theScore Bet) and
- *milestone "N+" ladders*, a Yes-only price per strike ("10+ Points", "To Score 20+ Points", "100+ Receiving Yards"): FanDuel, Fanatics, DraftKings, BetMGM, BetRivers, Unibet, Stoiximan, Bovada. NBA points alone: 1,877 Fanatics rows, 933 FanDuel, 624 Stoiximan, 485 DraftKings (`line_type: milestone`).
Novig lists the same strikes (an "N+" row is Over N - 0.5).

**2. What the engine did with it.** `PropLineClient.props` keeps a row only when it is a two-sided Over/Under at one point: every milestone row is thrown away ("a milestone rung has no second side to devig"). The scan then prices a Novig strike only when 2+ books post EXACTLY that strike two-sided (`Planner.lineStats`, `FairValue.minBooks` 2, BLEND). On the real boards (4,485 Novig prop strikes, fair 10-90%): **36% qualify** (NBA 32%, NHL 73%, NFL 27%). The maker's fair was the scan's, so 64% of prop legs had "no fair line" (the 23-27% the journals showed for NBA props within 3 h).

**3. Counting every price.** One vote per company ([BookCompany]): each two-sided row's devigged over probability, and each "N+" row's implied probability divided by that book's own margin (the median of its "N+" price over the other books' two-sided consensus at shared strikes, 1.05-1.14, measured at start up from the very boards; 1.07 when a book has too few). The strike's fair = the mean of the votes. Run through the engine's OWN parser and `PropLadderFair` on the same real boards (`PropLadderRealBoardsTest`, opt-in `VIGILANT_PROPBOARDS=`): **99% of Novig's strikes have a vote, 79% have 3+ companies, mean absolute error against Novig's two-sided mid 2.2 probability points** (4+ votes: NBA 1.8, NHL 1.4, NFL 2.4; one or two votes 3-5 points, which is why the maker still wants `minBooks` 3 companies). The estimate runs about 1.9 points above Novig's mid, the same direction as every prop fair here (the maker's 20% prop premium covers it).

**4. What was built.** `RefMilestone` + `RefEvent.milestones` (kept apart: nothing in the scan reads them), `PlMarket.milestones` in the PropLine parser (also through the board merge and the props source's per-game copies), `PropLadderFair` (calibrate, build, `atStrike`, `Index`), `RfqLadder.Fairs(consensus = ...)` consulted after the scan's own fair and before interpolation, `CloudMain` (rebuilt every minute from the props the scan already holds; paired with Novig's events by `Planner.matchEvents`; no extra request), switch `ladderVotes` (default on; `rfq_set.py ladderVotes=false` reverts), engine log line `RFQ prop votes: ...`. A leg priced this way is a prop leg like any: the prop premium (20% per leg, at most two prop legs), the 3-companies rule, the age rule apply. Tests: `PropLadderFairTest`, `RfqLadderTest`.

**5. Not done / next.** ParlayAPI's own prop ladders (same idea, not read yet); `*_PROTECTED` markets (void at fair market value when the player does not play) never match their unprotected ladder; game-line alternates (spreads/totals) from the books' alternate rows; the 20% prop premium and the two-prop limit decide how many of the newly priced legs become quotes: read the journal rows whose leg label starts "ladder-votes" against the market's EXECUTED prices before moving them.
