**Honest reading.** The naive standard errors (about 1%) count 1,200 parlays as independent when they are built from ~270 distinct legs that win or lose together. Redrawn with one outcome per distinct leg (`tools/rfq_shape_study.py`, 4,000 draws), the profile's +6.2% sits 1.1 spreads above luck and no excluded pocket is below -1.2 spreads. **Nothing here is proven; three slates cannot prove a shape.** The profile is the best current estimate, not a certainty: it avoids every pocket with a negative point estimate (a leg of 80%+, 3+ legs, parlays over .65), keeps the class with the best estimate, and is judged daily (SHAPES.txt) with 20+ independent settled fills of our own (count clusters of parlays that share a leg, not parlays).
Mechanism, which is why the point estimates are believed at all: parlay buyers who stack two moderate favourites are the classic parlay customer, the market's maker margin on a 2-leg parlay is the largest relative to its variance, and heavy-favourite legs (80%+) are where sharp money and favourite-longshot bias bite. The old sample of our 20 settled fills (-$63.42) was exactly the excluded pockets.
Also measured, no signal: the buyer's accept delay and the requested stake (no sharp-versus-casual difference), no buyer id on any row.

**Fills.** A 2-leg parlay of two favourites prices at .25-.64: collateral $6-$30 per $10 stake against $23-$90+ for the longshot parlays the old settings preferred, so the same wallet carries 2-4 times the fills. The old `onlyToday` filter declined 77% of the qualifying 2-leg RFQs overnight (their games start after the slate ends); `longshotsOnly` at 40% declined all of the +12% class priced over .40. **Margin:** at fair + 0.1 point the quote wins about 78% of the auctions of the best class (fair + 0.5: 51%, + 1.0: 25%) while profit per fill is the class edge either way; the margin is not a lever, the class is.

## 5. Is it the margin or the shape? (PF2, Tj 19:0x UTC: "The other Claude session said that the margin is not what makes parlays profitable for the maker, it is the types of parlays made. Verify this")
**Verified.** Same graded table (6,471 executed pregame parlays, our fair known for each; `tools/rfq_shape_study.py`, section "Margin or shape?" of `SHAPES.txt`), maker P&L per $ of pot:
- **Shape moves profit by tens of points, margin by almost none.** Mean maker ROI by shape (legs x a leg >= .8 x all legs >= .5) runs from -25.5% (4+ legs, a heavy leg, all favourites) to +12.1% (2 legs, no heavy leg, both legs >= .5); by the market's margin over our fair (1-point buckets) it runs -14% to +4% with no order (the biggest margins are the WORST: -8.2% at 2+ points, -6.9% at 3+: the buyer picks parlays where our fair is wrong). Of the variance of per-parlay P&L the shape explains 8.0%, the margin 0.7%.
- **A bad shape is not rescued by margin.** Executed parlays priced at least k points over our fair: 3 legs -9.7% / -13.8% / -12.3% / -9.9% at k = 0 / .5 / 1 / 2; 3+ legs all >= .5: -14.4% / -14.6% / -11.8% / -8.4%; a leg >= .8: -13.6% / -14.6% / -12.5% / -10.4%. No margin tested turns any of them positive.
- **A good shape does not need margin; margin costs fills.** If we quoted our fair + m points on every parlay of the profile class: m = -1 / -0.5 / +0.1 / +0.5 / +1 / +2 wins 95% / 91% / 65% / 30% / 13% / 3% of the auctions, the ROI of what we win is +5.6% / +5.8% / +6.7% / +9.2% / +7.9% / -4.3%, and the expected profit per RFQ entered is +5.3% / +5.3% / +4.4% / +2.7% / +1.0% / -0.1% of the pot (both-favourites class: +10.4 / +10.6 / +9.0 / +6.0 / +2.4 / +0.1). The profit per fill is the class's edge, almost independent of the margin; the margin only decides how many fills there are. So the engine stays at the floors (fair + about 0.1 point: `margin` .001, `minPoints` .0005, `fairErrorRel` .001, `minReturn` .001): below fair would add fills but cuts the safety against a wrong fair, and the point estimate gain (+21%) is inside the noise; above it halves the profit.
- Caveats: only parlays that executed (the sample the market selected), three slates and 270 distinct legs behind 1,200 parlays: shape orderings are point estimates (section 2); the margin finding is robust because it holds in every shape.
