# College basketball against-the-spread system, and parlays on Novig (NC1, 2026-11-05)

Tj, 2026-11-05 (typed): he used to log each college basketball team's points per game and whether it covered the spread (a tie within a few points, a green plus for a cover by a few, a double
plus for a cover by a lot; red minuses the other way), compared each day's matchups, and "this method actually worked". Build it for college basketball only: log scores and closing spreads on GitHub,
show the day's schedule with `Davidson (74 ++)` beside each team and Novig's spread and total, find the best bets and the spreads and totals "most unlike the averages", bet buttons like the +EV scanner,
a game sheet with other books and EV, drop-downs of Novig's alternate numbers, no outliers from small-school games, and "figure out if parlays can be made using the novig api".

Data source (probed 2026-11-05): ESPN's scoreboard (`site.api.espn.com/.../mens-college-basketball/scoreboard?dates=&groups=50`) gives scores and the schedule; its core odds feed
(`sports.core.api.espn.com/.../events/{id}/competitions/{id}/odds`) keeps the OPENING and CLOSING spread and total of every finished game that had a line. **ESPN changed provider mid-season 2025-26**
(ESPN BET to DraftKings, early December), so the tool takes the first pregame provider with a closing block (DraftKings, then ESPN BET) and never the "Live Odds" feed. Games with no line (non-Division I
opponents, exhibitions) are not logged: that is the "small school" filter. Coverage: 73 of 82 games on 2025-11-15. Closing lines are the sharpest price there is, so a test against them is the hardest honest test.

## The method as built (Tj's words, made exact)

- A team's cover margin in a game = its margin + its closing spread (-6.5 favourite winning by 9 = +2.5). |margin| under 3 is a tie (0), 3 to 10 a plus or minus (1), 10 and over a double (2). A team's
  rating is the mean tier; its symbol is `++` from 1.2, `+` from 0.4, `=` between, `-` and `--` below -0.4 and -1.2. Points per game is the plain average.
- Left out of every average: games with a spread of 20+ points (a big school against a small one; the underdog's score is deflated as much as the favourite's is inflated), and, for the points only, any
  score more than 2 robust deviations from the team's median (from 6 games; the deviation is 1.4826 x the median absolute deviation, at least 6 points). A team shows after 3 usable games.
- Nothing is looked at from the future: a game's ratings use only that team's earlier games of the same season.

## Does the method beat the closing line? No. Three seasons, 14,669 games

Baselines on these games: home covers 49.6%, the favourite 49.7%, the Over 50.0%. A -110 price needs 52.4%; a side near even money costs about 50.5 cents on Novig. Signals and what the side each
names did (full tables: `research/ncaab/backtest_2026-11-05.md`):

| Signal (as Tj described it) | Games | Named side won | Profit per $1 at -110 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Back the team ahead on scoring AND on covering ("your rule", the A-vs-B examples) | 3,463 | **49.1%** | -6.2% |
| Back the side the scoring margins favour more than the line does | 14,658 | 50.2% | -4.2% |
| Total: the teams' scoring against the line, Over/Under by the gap | 14,657 | **48.8%** | -6.9% |
| Back the team with the better green-plus average | 14,359 | 48.4% | -7.6% |
| **Fade it** (back the team with the WORSE cover average) | 14,359 | **51.6%** | -1.4% |

- Same result with 10+ games per team (51.8% for the fade, 49.6% for the rule), and by season (the fade: 51.6%, 50.6%, 52.8%; the rule: 51.2%, 49.3%, 47.0%).
- The one pocket that looked good on 2025-26 alone, the averages-vs-line gap of 3 to 6 points at 54.2% over 1,474 games, was 49-50% in the two older seasons (51.2% over the three): noise found by
  looking at five buckets of four signals.
- The fade is the only signal distinguishable from a coin flip (z = +3.9) and it stays under -110's break-even. At Novig's ~50.5 cent price its edge is +2% at best: thin, and the 95% bound
  (`NcaabSignals.lower95`) is what the app calls "backed by history". It is the OPPOSITE of the method: teams that have covered a lot tend to cool off (the market already prices the streak).
- Possible reasons the method worked for Tj: earlier or softer lines than the close, a different pool of games, or the ordinary luck of a few hundred bets (the standard error of a win rate over 300 bets
  is 2.9 points). The app records this season's results beside every bucket (`nc`/`rc` in `model.json`), so the claim is tested on fresh games as they finish.

What the app does with it: builds everything Tj asked for, ranks "Leans" strongest-first by his method, and prints beside each one what that kind of lean did. `Reading.backed` (95% sure to beat 50.5 cents,
100+ games, both halves of the sample above 50%) is the only thing that earns a check mark; today only the faded streak can. Tj's rule is shown honestly as an opinion.

## Parlays on Novig: can they be made with the API?

- **Pricing: yes, with no key.** `POST https://api.novig.com/rfq/v2/quote/unauthenticated {"outcomeIds":[...]}` (2 to 20 outcomes) answers `{"price":"0.56","expiresAt":...,"serverNow":...}`: the parlay's implied
  probability, good for about 30 seconds. 1 request a second per address, a burst of 10. Errors: 400 with the reason ("A parlay must include two or more legs.", "Invalid parlay combination."), 404 no price now,
  429 with Retry-After. A browser-style user agent is needed (CloudFront refused Python's default).
- **Placing: no, not through the trading API.** Parlays are filled by liquidity providers answering RFQs (`/api-reference/rfq/*`: a separate LP section, W-9 and a deposit; NOVIG_API.md section 14), and
  `/v3/orders` takes single outcomes. The documented way in is a deep link: `novigapp://events/<id>,<id>,<id>/<partner>/<amount>` opens the slip with every leg filled in (docs.novig.com/affiliates/deeplinking).
  So the app prices the parlay and opens Novig's slip; the tap that places it is Tj's, in Novig.
- **The price is good.** 8 two-leg samples (2026-11-05, NCAAB money lines, books read the same minute): the quote equalled the product of the legs' MIDDLE prices within 0.5 points and ran 3 to 4 points of
  probability BELOW stacking the legs' take prices (the cost of crossing the spread twice):

  | Legs (cost of taking each, multiplied) | Product of mids | Novig's parlay quote |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.6006 | 0.5598 | 0.560 |
  | 0.3595 | 0.3252 | 0.323 |
  | 0.5063 | 0.4760 | 0.478 |
  | 0.1403 | 0.1190 | 0.118 |
  | 0.2310 | 0.2012 | 0.202 |
  | 0.5852 | 0.5438 | 0.545 |
  | 0.4116 | 0.3912 | 0.396 |
  | 0.1029 | 0.0888 | 0.088 |

  A parlay of +EV legs therefore keeps more of its edge than the same bets taken one by one. Caveats: one afternoon, one sport, the quote lasts 30 s and the liquidity provider has a last look; legs from
  different games are independent, same-game legs are not (Novig refuses two legs of one kind in a game and two games that share a team: `NovigParlay.problem` checks the same rules first).
- Built: the parlay builder in the NCAAB tab (add legs from a game's sheet, price it at Novig, stacked-straights comparison, EV vs the books when every leg has a book price, Open in Novig).

## Maintaining it

- `ncaab-log.yml` seeds `ncaab-data` from `research/ncaab/seed/` on its first run and then logs the last 3 days each run; dispatch with `from`/`to` to backfill, `backtest=true` to re-run the backtest now
  (it also runs weekly). Rebuild the golden cases after changing a signal: `python3 -I tools/ncaab_ats.py golden --data-dir data/src/test/resources/ncaab` (the fast check fails when they are stale).
- A new signal goes into `SIGNALS` and `signal_value` in the tool, `NcaabSignals.value` and the golden file; it earns nothing until its bucket table beats Novig's price with 95% confidence.
- Not built: Novig's own closing lines in the log (the engine's `lines-*.csv.gz` already hold them for NCAAB; a join would test the same signals against Novig's close), an engine lane that bets the backed
  leans (it would need the class B gates in `research/review/ROUTINE.md`: only the faded streak qualifies and its edge is about 2%).
