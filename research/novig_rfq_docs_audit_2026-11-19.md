## 1. Verdict
The maker's frames, signing, subscriptions and settlement maths matched the docs. **Six real gaps were found and fixed**, one rule was misread in a comment, and the docs' "10 sockets" turned out to be most useful for one thing: protecting the 1-second last look.

| # | Gap (doc rule) | What the code did | Fix (MK13) |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | "Reconnect with backoff" (socket); on a `451` "stop and fix the cause before you retry; a client that keeps retrying a 451 can get the account locked with a 423" (errors) | The upgrade failure arrives on the websocket listener, not as an exception, so **the only backoff was a fixed 1-second loop**: a refused upgrade (429 `TOO_MANY_SOCKETS`, 451, 423, 401, 403) or a socket closed for a revoked key / refused location was retried every second, each upgrade spending 32 tokens of the subaccount's `stream` throttle shared with the market feed | `RfqReconnect.plan`: doubling 2 s -> 60 s after every failure; `TOO_MANY_SOCKETS` 30 s+ (a socket with a missed Pong is closed within about 15-30 s); `Retry-After` honoured; **451 / 423 / 401 / 403 and close 1008 `KEY_REVOKED` / `KEY_EXPIRED` / location: 10-30 minutes**; 1009: 1 minute; the JSON `code` of a refused upgrade is read from the body; a socket stable for 60 s resets the count (`RfqReconnectTest`, `RfqSocketRulesTest` against a mock) |
| 2 | "A dropped connection before you answer rejects it ... every rejected or missed last look counts against your confirm rate" (socket, confirm-rate) | The job ended (6-hour hand-over, reset, stall) by killing the process: **every quote still out lost its last look**, counted against the 99% bar, and the next job opened its socket minutes later. Users accept 4-29 s after a quote (14 fills measured) | `RfqMaker.beginDrain()` at the end of the job: no new quotes, sockets stay open until 45 s after the last quote (at most 60 s); nothing to wait for in SHADOW |
| 3 | "Novig queues 256 frames per connection and DROPS EVENT FRAMES when it is full" and `quote_accepted` is an event frame with a 1-second window (socket, "Timing and limits") | One socket carried the 60-RFQs-a-second firehose and the last look; a stall of two seconds on our side (a GC pause, a slow journal write) fills the queue | **A second socket on the same key subscribes to `quotes` ONLY** (LIVE): a handful of frames, never a full queue. The docs allow 10 sockets per key. Misread rule fixed in a comment: `quote_accepted` carries "your quoted price" (not a price Novig chooses), and the 1-second window is per last look, not per connection.

## 2. What the 10 sockets are good for (and what they are not)
- **Used: a quiet last-look socket** (gap 3). Pricing socket: `rfq` + `quotes` + `executions` (as before). Last-look socket (LIVE only): `quotes` only. If Novig delivers `quote_accepted` to every socket of the maker ("quotes events only to you"), the quiet socket answers inside the window even when the pricing socket is busy; if it delivers only to the socket that quoted, the quiet socket stays idle and nothing is lost. **Unverified until the first live quote** (SHADOW sends no quote): check `status.rfq.sockets` and the journal's `CONFIRM` rows (`dup:true` marks the second delivery) in the first live minutes.
- Not used: sharding the pricing (60 RFQs a second is a few milliseconds of work a second; two `rfq` sockets would only deliver every RFQ twice, which the maker now ignores). A read-only socket (`trading::read`) for SHADOW and the probe is possible (the key and the subaccount are the same maker) but SHADOW cannot quote by construction; not worth another key.
- Costs: every upgrade spends 32 `stream` tokens (capacity 512 on this key at BASE), so two sockets are 64, and the back-off in gap 1 is what keeps a bad day from draining the bucket the market feed shares.

## 3. A dedicated maker subaccount (docs: "a subaccount is a separate wallet")
`api/concepts/account-model`: each subaccount is "a separate wallet: balance, positions, orders", up to 10 live; `api/throttling`: "each subaccount keeps its own throttles, so each one gets the full limits of your tier"; `api/rfq/makers`: "To run more than one maker, open more subaccounts and register each one"; the 10 sockets are per KEY. The maker currently quotes from the Vigilant subaccount, so its collateral competes with the engine's bets and bids for one balance, its `stream`/`account` throttles are shared with the market feed, and its profit is mixed into the wallet's. **Recommendation for when Tj adds money: open a second subaccount, fund it with the maker's bankroll only, and send its `trading` key as the optional secrets `NOVIG_MAKER_KEY_ID` / `NOVIG_MAKER_KEY_PEM`** (the engine already reads them: `CloudKeys.MAKER`, `cloud-scan.yml`, `rfqClient` in `CloudMain`; without them nothing changes). Opening and funding a subaccount moves money: Tj's, with his management key (class C in research/review/ROUTINE.md); no session does it. Benefits: the maker's P&L is its wallet's change, a maker loss stop cannot starve bids, the engine's wallet-use caps no longer have to guess what the parlays hold, and the maker's own 10 sockets and buckets.

## 5. Still open (deviations kept, with why)
1. **`description` and outcome names are "display text, don't parse it"** (universe model; outcome `name`: "Not a contract"). The repo parses them to read a player (`NovigText.subjectOf`) and a side (`Over`), and the MK11 ladder groups prop rungs that way; the planner cross-checks a line against `strike` where it can. There is no id for a player, only `subjectId` on team outcomes (changelog 2026-11-11); until Novig adds one, parsing stays, and a leg whose description does not parse is not priced.
2. **Participation Protection** (`*_PROTECTED` twin markets, QA on 2026-11-12, production date unset): a protected prop that the player leaves after halftime resolves at its pregame price (a void at FMV), a standard one voids only when the player never plays. Our fair for either is the books' price of the standard prop; the protected twin is priced like a prop (declined unless `propPremium` allows). Re-read when Novig sets a production date.
3. `quote_accepted` routing across sockets (section 2): verify in the first live minutes.
4. The 99% bar counts "from your first last look": the first few live last looks carry the most weight (a lock came at 11 counted last looks on 2026-11-19; `minSample` is in `GET /rfq/v3/maker`). The first live session should start with a handful of quotes and read the standing after each.
5. The OpenAPI file lists only the paper host (`api.paper.novig.com`); production serves the same routes (verified since 2026-11-18).
